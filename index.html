01 · Theoretical Background

What is a share buyback?

A company reacquires its own shares from current shareholders.

Process

Buyback

The company buys back its own shares from current shareholders. Also called share repurchase.
Result

Treasury shares

The repurchased shares sit on the company's balance sheet as treasury shares.
Effect

Cash out

The company distributes cash to shareholders in exchange for their shares. The capital flow is the opposite of an IPO.
Market

Demand

Repurchases create additional stock demand, so share prices may rise.

Ways to conduct share buybacks

MethodHow it works
Open-market repurchase (most common)The company announces its intention and buys shares on the public stock market.
Public tender offerAn offer to repurchase a set number of shares at a fixed price or within a price range. Mainly addressed to smaller investors.
Repurchase from large investorsShares are bought directly from a large shareholder.
DerivativesUse of derivative instruments to structure the repurchase.

Historical development

Before the 1980s, companies running repurchase programmes faced a real risk of legal disputes. The main concerns were information asymmetries and stock price manipulation. There were fewer than 30 annual U.S. repurchase announcements.

1982: U.S. authorities introduced Rule 10b-18, a safe-harbour rule. Companies acting according to it were legally protected. U.S. spending on buybacks tripled within one year.

Since then, many countries have deregulated, and global repurchase activity has increased significantly.

02 · Motives

Why do companies buy back shares?

From outside the company, the actual motive is hard to identify. The potential motives are below.

EPS

Increase earnings per share

Fewer shares lift EPS, which helps meet or beat analyst forecasts.
Dilution

Offset dilution

Counters the share count growth caused by stock-based compensation.
Signal

Signaling

The legally required announcement communicates that the stock may be undervalued. This can raise investor demand.
Timing

Market timing

The company repurchases below fair value, which can be a good investment.
Cash

Excess cash

An alternative when there is excess cash or few investment opportunities.

03 · Advantages and Shortcomings

For whom is a buyback good?

Advantages

Company

High flexibility

Buybacks are a flexible way to distribute earnings. The announcement is not binding, so the company can still decide not to repurchase.
Company

Timing control

The company decides when the cash goes out. Lower commitment than dividends, which can create long-term payment expectations.
Shareholder

Tax advantage

Gains are often taxed at lower rates than dividends. Tax applies only when shares are sold, while dividends are taxed directly.
Market

Short-term support

Potential short-term support for the stock price and for EPS targets.

Shortcomings and risks

Own addition

Who pays?

The cash outflow can cut current investments or prevent necessary long-term investments. Short-term choices can come at the expense of the future.

Case study: S&P 500, 2003 to 2012

449 U.S. companies distributed their earnings as follows:

Share repurchases54%
Dividends37%
Retained in the company9%

Part of the repurchases was financed by debt, which weakens the financial position further. Higher default risk can lower bond prices and ratings, which hurts bondholders.

05 · Empirical Findings

Hybrid payouts and share counts

I. Mixed payout model

DAX and MDAX companies are moving from a strict dividend focus to a flexible hybrid model that increasingly includes buybacks. Dividends signal financial health, while buybacks offer an alternative to return capital without committing to long-term dividend increases.

II. Strategic capital allocation

Companies with high free cash flow use buybacks to keep capital discipline when local investment returns in Germany and the EU are low. Combined dividends and buybacks prioritise EPS and investor returns.

06 · Conclusion

Strong capital markets, hybrid payouts

Macro

Capital decoupling

Germany's domestic economy contrasts with its booming capital markets. The DAX rally is sustained by global revenue streams rather than domestic productivity.
Mix

Hybrid payout mix

Dividends remain higher than buybacks, but buybacks are becoming more relevant.
Return

Return intensity

Between 7.4% and 29.8% of EBITDA was returned to shareholders from 2021 to 2025.
Discipline

Capital discipline

More companies cut their share count (30.26%) than increased it (28.95%).
Trade-off

The growth-for-payout trade-off

Multi-billion-euro payout programmes move capital away from R&D and future reinvestment. Shareholder distributions and long-term growth therefore compete for the same money.

Own addition: regulation in Germany and the EU

Own addition

Germany: AktG §71

The Stock Corporation Act generally limits repurchases to 10% of share capital, and the shares must be paid for from distributable funds.
Own addition

EU: MAR safe harbour

The EU Market Abuse Regulation (Art. 5 MAR) and its delegated regulation set conditions, such as reporting, price and volume limits, that protect companies buying back shares.
